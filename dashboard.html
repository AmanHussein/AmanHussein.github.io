What the results suggest

Across all ten products, linear regression covered 8 additional units, while average total ending stock increased by 0.33 units. For HOBBIES_1_008, lower forecast error coincided with 7 more unmet units. Forecast accuracy and replenishment outcomes need to be assessed together.

I would retain the moving average as the default while testing the regression over more periods and with realistic lead times.

Method and simulation assumptions

One pooled linear regression was trained on weeks before 1 February 2016. Inputs were the previous week's sales and the preceding four-week sales average. Each test forecast is one week ahead and can use sales observed in earlier test weeks. The trained coefficients remain fixed.

Each product starts with 3 units. The order raises available stock to the rounded-up forecast plus 1 safety unit when needed. Unmet demand is lost and does not carry forward. Ending inventory carries to the following week.

Recorded sales are a proxy for demand and can understate demand during actual stockouts. The zero-sales week for HOBBIES_1_008 is retained with unknown cause. Ending stock is measured at week end and differs from average inventory held during the week. These results cover one store, ten products and twelve weeks.
